TrailTracker · Your privacy

Privacy Policy

TrailTracker helps you document outdoor trips while keeping your recorded trails and photos on your device, under your control.

Provided by Otto Sponring · Updated November 21, 2026

Privacy at a glance

TrailTracker does not store your recorded trips or photos on a developer-operated website or cloud server. Your saved records are stored locally in the Trails folder you select on your device. Viewing public map imagery involves contacting a third-party map provider, as explained below.

1. What TrailTracker does

With your permission, TrailTracker uses location information made available by Android to record a trip. Depending on your device and conditions, positioning may draw on satellite navigation systems including GPS, GLONASS, and BeiDou, along with other location sources supported by your phone. TrailTracker does not control which satellite systems Android uses.

  • Records a trail's location points, route, distance traveled, direction, altitude, and elapsed time.
  • Displays the distance back to the original starting point, in addition to the cumulative length of the traveled path. These are different measurements.
  • Allows you to select your mode of transportation for the trip.
  • Lets you capture photos associated with your trail.
  • Can continue tracking while the screen is off, with an Android tracking notification.

Position measurements and calculated distances are estimates and may vary with device capabilities, terrain, signal conditions, and location accuracy.

2. Permissions and information used

Location: Precise location permission is needed to record trails and compute trip information. Where supported and permitted, the app may continue recording while the screen is off.

Camera: Camera permission is used when you choose TrailTracker’s built-in photo capture. Holding the Photo control opens its preview; releasing the control attempts to capture a photo. A successful capture is saved automatically, without a keep-or-delete prompt. You can instead use your device’s Camera app for additional camera controls while keeping your TrailTracker trip running.

Files: You choose the destination folder through Android's system folder picker. On Android 9 and earlier, storage permission may be needed to share older saved files.

3. Where your information is stored

During a trip, the current route is maintained in device memory. When you stop a trip containing location points, TrailTracker saves it as a GPX file in the Main Storage/Trails folder selected on your device. The filename contains the local date and time when recording stopped. Older files may remain in Documents/Trails.

Every photo successfully captured using TrailTracker’s built-in photo control is saved automatically as a separate file in the selected Trails folder; TrailTracker does not display a keep-or-delete confirmation after capture. Photos taken with your device’s separate Camera app are managed and stored according to that app’s settings, which may use a different folder. When available, the app attempts to embed the last recorded trail location at the time of capture in the photo's location metadata and mark it as a waypoint in the GPX trail. A photo may be saved without location metadata if no trail position is available. If updating metadata fails, an original copy may remain; check the photo's location before sharing it. Photo image data is not included inside the GPX file.

Saved GPX files and photos are not automatically deleted when you start another trip, restart your device, or uninstall the app. Files in shared storage may be accessible to other apps that have appropriate permissions. You can review or delete saved files later using your device’s file manager or a compatible photo app. TrailTracker does not ask whether to keep or delete a successfully captured photo.

4. Public maps and internet connections

Preview displays a snapshot of the current trail without stopping tracking. The app writes a temporary GPX snapshot in its private cache and attempts to delete it after loading the map. A temporary file may remain if cleanup fails or the app is interrupted; Android may clear cached files later. Preview does not create a saved trail; stopping a trip containing location points saves the permanent GPX file. Photo processing also uses a temporary private cache copy, which the app attempts to delete after processing.

When you preview or open a saved trail in map view, TrailTracker can display it over public map tiles from OpenStreetMap (Street map) or OpenTopoMap (Terrain map). The route is drawn from the GPX file stored on your device; the GPX file itself is not uploaded to these map providers.

To display a map area, your device requests tiles over the internet. The map provider may receive technical information such as your IP address and the geographic area represented by the requested tiles. Map tiles may be cached temporarily on your device; their availability and retention depend on caching behavior. The map requires an internet connection whenever the needed tiles are not already cached.

Important: “Temporary map display” does not mean that a third-party map provider is unable to log tile requests. Those providers have their own privacy and retention practices. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation Privacy Policy and OpenTopoMap website for provider information.

5. Uploads, sharing, and third parties

TrailTracker does not upload your recorded trail files, photos, or location history to the developer and does not use third-party advertising networks or analytics services. The only advertisement in the app is a book promotion for a publication by the developer. It does not maintain an online account or a cloud-hosted archive of your trails.

If you deliberately open or share a GPX file or photo with another app or service, the recipient may receive the contents, including location coordinates and photo metadata. The receiving app or service is subject to its own privacy practices. Separately, Android and your device's location provider may process location information under their own settings and policies.

6. Your choices and control

  • Start, pause, or stop recording within TrailTracker.
  • Change or revoke location and camera permissions in Android settings.
  • Select the Trails folder used to save records.
  • View, export, or delete saved GPX files and photos later using your device’s file manager or compatible apps.
  • Use your device’s Camera app when you want additional photo controls, without ending your active TrailTracker trip. The Camera app’s own storage and privacy practices apply.
  • Avoid opening online map views if you do not want map tile requests sent to public map providers.

Starting a new trip clears the previous trail from the app's display but does not remove saved files. If there is no current trip, the app may display a sample trail; it is demonstration data, not your location.
